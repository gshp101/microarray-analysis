PathRx · in your browser

From a GEO microarray to affected pathways

Search GEO for a two-group microarray study, label the samples, then run each PathRx step and look at what it produced before moving on. The Python runs on this page with Pyodide; nothing is sent to a server except the downloads from public databases.

Read the tutorial: the whole pipeline worked through on a liver cancer study, with what each step does and why.

Starting Python…
Data sources and KEGG access

GEO, Reactome, Enrichr (LINCS L1000, DepMap) and the protein network all allow requests from a web page. KEGG does not: rest.kegg.jp sends no CORS headers, so browsers block it. Without KEGG, the KEGG pathway sets, the drug-target route and network proximity are skipped. To include them, run the page with python web/serve.py (it forwards /kegg/ to KEGG), or point this at your own KEGG proxy. KEGG data is free for academic use and needs a license for commercial use.

Downloads are kept in this browser between visits.
01

Find a dataset

Searches GEO DataSets for human series profiled by microarray. Pick one to load, or type an accession you already know.

02

Load the series

Pick a dataset first.

Downloads the series matrix (normalized values and sample descriptions) and the platform annotation that maps each probe to a gene. Large studies are tens of megabytes.

03

Define the groups

Load a series first.

Each group is a regular expression matched, ignoring case, against every sample's title, source and characteristics. Samples that match neither group, or both, are left out. Click a value below to add it to the selected group.

04

Differential expression

Define the groups first.

For every gene: log2 fold change (case mean minus control mean), Welch's t-test, and Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate.

05

Pathway gene sets

Run differential expression first.

Reactome pathways under "Metabolism" (and KEGG's metabolism maps, when KEGG is reachable). Sets made mostly of ribosome, proteasome or nuclear pore genes are dropped because they track housekeeping, not metabolism.

06

Score pathways

Load gene sets first.

Two methods. GSEA ranks every gene by its t statistic and asks whether a pathway's genes bunch up at the top or bottom; it catches pathways where many genes move a little. ORA asks whether the significant up (or down) genes fall in a pathway more often than chance.

07

Drug leads

Score pathways first.

Takes the genes that drive the shifted pathways and finds existing drugs two ways: drugs acting on those genes in the direction that would push them back (KEGG DRUG), and compounds whose LINCS L1000 expression signature is the opposite of the disease. These are research leads, not treatment advice.

08

Network proximity

Run drug leads first.

Ranks KEGG drugs by how close their targets sit to the pathway-driving genes in the human protein interaction network, against random protein sets of the same size and connectivity (Guney et al. 2016). Negative z means closer than chance.

09

Cell line dependencies

Run drug leads first.

For cancer data only. Flags pathway-driving genes that DepMap cancer cell lines of the same tissue need to grow, more often than other cell lines do. Skip it for other diseases.

10

Report

Score pathways first.

The same self-contained HTML report the command line writes, from the steps run so far.

PathRx is MIT licensed. Source: github.com/gshp101/microarray-analysis. GSEA here is a NumPy port of the preranked method; on GSE14520 it gives the same enrichment scores as gseapy and NES within about 0.2.
